# Claim History Agent

### AI-Powered Historical Claims Analysis for Insurance Underwriting

**Part of the InsureIQ Multi-Agent Underwriting Platform**

---

# Overview

The Claim History Agent is a specialized AI agent responsible for evaluating an applicant's historical insurance claims before a policy is underwritten. It forms an integral part of the Research Stage within the InsureIQ underwriting workflow.

Historical claims often provide valuable insight into future insurance risk. Applicants with frequent claims, recurring incidents, or significant historical losses generally present a different level of underwriting exposure than applicants with a stable claims history.

The Claim History Agent automates this review by analysing historical claim information, identifying behavioural patterns, assessing historical loss experience, and producing a structured claim risk assessment.

Instead of manually reviewing years of historical records, underwriters receive a concise, explainable summary that highlights the most relevant findings and supports informed decision-making.

---

# Purpose

The objective of the Claim History Agent is to determine whether an applicant's previous insurance claims indicate an increased likelihood of future losses.

Rather than focusing on individual incidents, the agent evaluates historical behaviour across multiple claims to identify meaningful trends that may influence underwriting decisions.

---

# Where the Agent Fits

The Claim History Agent is executed after the Audit Agent has completed document validation and created the structured underwriting case.

During the Research Stage, it runs in parallel with the Financial Risk Agent and Operational Risk Agent, allowing all three assessments to be completed simultaneously.

After completing its analysis, the generated findings are forwarded to the Summary Agent, where they become part of the final underwriting assessment.

# Analysis Performed

The Claim History Agent evaluates several aspects of historical insurance performance.

## Claim Frequency

Measures how often insurance claims have occurred over previous policy periods.

Applicants with repeated claims generally require closer underwriting attention.

---

## Claim Severity

Evaluates the financial impact of previous losses.

A small number of high-value claims may represent greater underwriting exposure than multiple low-value incidents.

---

## Historical Loss Trend

Reviews how claim activity has changed over time.

The agent determines whether claim frequency and loss amounts are increasing, decreasing, or remaining stable.

---

## Recurring Claim Categories

Repeated claims within the same category often indicate unresolved operational issues.

Examples include:

- Cargo Damage
- Machinery Failure
- Theft
- Fire
- Collision
- Environmental Incidents

---

## Behavioural Pattern Analysis

The agent also identifies long-term behavioural trends such as:

- Repeated operational failures
- Similar claim causes
- Seasonal incidents
- Recurring loss locations
- High-risk operational practices

---

# Risk Assessment

The collected observations are combined to generate an overall Claim Risk Score.

Example classification:

| Score | Category |
|-------:|----------|
| 0 – 30 | Low Risk |
| 31 – 60 | Medium Risk |
| 61 – 100 | High Risk |

The calculated score is accompanied by supporting observations so that underwriters understand how the assessment was reached.

# Business Value

Historical insurance claims provide valuable evidence of an applicant's previous risk exposure. However, manually reviewing years of claim records can significantly increase underwriting time and introduce inconsistencies between assessments.

The Claim History Agent streamlines this process by automatically analysing historical claims, identifying meaningful trends, and generating an explainable risk assessment. This enables underwriters to quickly understand an applicant's historical claims profile without manually reviewing every record.

By incorporating historical claims intelligence into the underwriting workflow, the agent improves consistency, reduces manual effort, accelerates policy evaluation, and supports better-informed underwriting decisions.
